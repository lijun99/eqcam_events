What cameras can and cannot measure

Summary of three draft notes on the 2026-11-20 11:25:35 UTC Mw 3.94 event, 1 km NW of Commerce, CA: Camera motion vs seismic stations, Camera mount model: Mount Wilson and A cable as a seismometer. 2026-11-24.

In one sentence. The image motion of public cameras is not a scientific measurement of the ground motion; cameras add visual evidence of damage and effects, and points to the intensity map, and some objects in their views carry partial information on the shaking.

What we did

  • Compared the image motion of five cameras with the nearest seismic stations, 0.13-4.6 km away: arrival times, envelopes and spectra.
  • Modeled the camera mount physically (a pole or mast as a damped oscillator driven by the ground) to see whether calibrating it outside earthquakes could remove its response, and tested this at Mount Wilson, 130 m from station CI.MWC.
  • Tracked two wires in the Skid Row view and compared their motion with station CI.USC, 4.6 km away.

What cameras can give

  • Whether a place shook, and when, to about 1-2 s once the stream delay is known. The envelope of the camera motion follows the station's (correlation 0.75-0.94).
  • Effects of the shaking: damage, falling objects, landslides, fire, smoke, power outages, people's reactions. This is intensity evidence, including in places without stations.
  • Partial information from objects in the view. A cable across the Skid Row view rang at its own mode (1.05 Hz) and gave the ground's amplitude at that frequency to within a factor of about 2, independent of the camera's mount. A wire along the view followed the 6 Hz horizontal shaking over time (correlation 0.88), without an amplitude scale.
  • Each camera's mount: its resonance and whether its motion is linear, from ambient clips. This helps to screen cameras.
  • Near a fault, the surface rupture itself, as in the 2025 Myanmar video.

What they cannot give

  • Magnitude, hypocenter, mechanism: these need seismic networks.
  • Ground-motion amplitudes or spectra from the camera's own motion. It is the ground filtered by an unknown mount, in pixels that depend on the zoom. The spectra did not match the stations' (shape correlation -0.6 to +0.4), even 130 m from one, where the camera's motion was impulsive (probably a loose part), not a linear response. PGA and PGV cannot be read from it.
  • Removing the mount by calibration. Ambient motion gives the mount's resonances, not its gain, and near a resonance the correction is very sensitive to the damping.
  • Precise P and S picks. P is weak or picked late, and the stream delays (0-7 s) are unknown without a clock in the video.
  • Frequencies above 7.5-15 Hz (half the frame rate). Video codec glitches add false pulses.

What would change this

  • A seismometer at or near a few cameras, to measure each mount's response once.
  • Stream clocks, or regular timing tests, for the delays.
  • Surveyed distances of the cables and wires in the views, so that their motion can be converted to ground motion.
  • Larger events and more cameras: all of this rests on one small event.

Station data: Southern California Earthquake Data Center (SCEDC); location: USGS ComCat (ci41364111). Camera motion: eqcam, from public camera streams. About, sources and removal requests
